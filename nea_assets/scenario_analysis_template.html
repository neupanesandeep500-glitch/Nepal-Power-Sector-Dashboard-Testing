Planning-Under-Uncertainty

Assign each enabled variant a likelihood (weights need not sum to 100 — they're normalized automatically) to get a probability-weighted expected outcome across the scenario set.

Monte Carlo Risk Simulation

Turns a single point estimate into a probability distribution — the same risk-quantification approach used in modern capacity-expansion and energy-transition planning. Two modes: a fast single-year snapshot (independent draws), or a full-horizon Markov-chain path where hydrology (source-side) and demand growth (load-side) each evolve as persistent, serially-correlated regimes year over year — so a drought or a growth slowdown can cluster across consecutive years instead of being re-randomized every year, which a purely deterministic or i.i.d. approach misses.

Persistence = probability a regime carries into the next year; higher = longer droughts/booms, lower = closer to independent year-to-year draws. States: hydrology {Wet, Normal, Dry, Severe Dry} shift generation ±12%/±24%; demand regime {High, Trend, Slow Growth} shift demand growth ±3 pp/yr. GDP, EV-adoption and induction-cooking assumptions are drawn once per run (scenario-level uncertainty) using the spreads below.
