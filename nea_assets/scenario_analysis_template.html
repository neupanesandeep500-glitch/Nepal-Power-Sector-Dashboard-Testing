Sensitivity — What Moves Net Revenue?

Each driver in the active variant is nudged up/down independently and the resulting swing in target-year net revenue is measured, holding everything else fixed.

Planning-Under-Uncertainty

Assign each enabled variant a likelihood (weights need not sum to 100 — they're normalized automatically) to get a probability-weighted expected outcome across the scenario set.

Stochastic Risk Simulation

Turns single point estimates into probability distributions across every major system variable — generation, demand, exports, reserve margin, surplus/deficit and financial return — the standard risk-quantification practice in modern capacity-expansion and energy-transition planning. Choose the method on the left: a fast independent-draw snapshot, or a full-horizon Markov-chain path where hydrology (source-side) and demand growth (load-side) persist as serially-correlated regimes year over year, so droughts and slowdowns can cluster realistically instead of being re-randomized every year.

Method
Regime Persistence

Higher persistence = longer droughts/booms. States: hydrology {Wet, Normal, Dry, Severe Dry} ±12%/±24% generation; demand {High, Trend, Slow} ±3 pp/yr.

Sampling

± spread (triangular half-width) for GDP/EV/induction sampling. Presets refill these; edit to fine-tune.

Target-Year Diagnosis & Required Adjustments

At the selected target year, checks the active variant against standard power-system planning thresholds — supply-adequacy reserve margin, transmission/grid headroom, and evacuation capacity — and where a gap exists, solves for the parameter value that would close it, holding everything else fixed. This turns a risk number into an actionable planning target rather than just a warning.

Model Limitations

Read before using these results for a real decision. This tool is a screening-level scenario model, not a production-grade capacity-expansion or unit-commitment study.

  1. No hourly/sub-annual dispatch. Generation, demand and trade are modelled as annual energy totals (GWh/MW), not an hourly or seasonal load-duration-curve dispatch. Monsoon-vs-dry-season hydro variation within a year, daily peak-shaving, and short-duration storage are not represented.
  2. No transmission network topology. "Grid capacity" and "evacuation capacity" are single aggregate MW limits, not a modelled network with individual line ratings, N-1 contingency, or losses that vary by corridor.
  3. Hydrology and demand-regime states are illustrative, not statistically calibrated. The Markov-chain shock magnitudes (±12%/±24% generation, ±3 pp/yr demand) and default persistence are reasoned assumptions, not fitted to Nepal's actual historical hydrology or business-cycle data. Treat probabilities as directional, not exact.
  4. Financial assumptions are simplified. A single flat domestic tariff, export price and import price apply across all consumer categories and the full horizon — no tariff escalation, no take-or-pay contract structures, no plant-level cost/depreciation/financing.
  5. No unit-level generation detail. Plant-by-plant availability, forced-outage rates, maintenance scheduling and commissioning-date risk for specific projects are not modelled — "capacity growth" is a single aggregate annual rate.
  6. Macro-economic uplift (GDP/EV/induction) is additive, not a full macro-econometric model. It captures a reasoned elasticity/incremental-load effect, not general-equilibrium feedback (e.g., higher tariffs suppressing demand, or generation investment affecting GDP).
  7. Export absorption is a single India-side assumption (a flat % of surplus), not a modelled cross-border market with its own price formation, seasonal demand, or third-country (Bangladesh) transactions.
  8. The goal-seek solver above holds every other parameter fixed when solving for a required value — it shows one feasible lever in isolation, not the jointly optimal combination of levers (which would require a formal optimization model, e.g. linear/mixed-integer programming).
